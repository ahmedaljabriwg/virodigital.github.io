Why it matters

Inaccessible sites cost you customers — and carry real risk.

Accessibility isn't just good practice. For many organizations it's a legal requirement and a condition of doing business.

ADA & legal exposure

Web-accessibility lawsuits and demand letters keep rising. An audit and a documented remediation plan are your best defense.

Section 508 mandates

Selling to government or working with agencies means conforming to Section 508 and providing a VPAT. No conformance, no contract.

Lost customers

Roughly 1 in 4 U.S. adults lives with a disability. If they can't navigate, read, or check out on your site, that's revenue walking away.

Why now

The deadlines are real — and remediation takes months.

Accessibility isn't a once-a-year filing, but the law now sets hard dates. A full audit and fix runs weeks to months — so the organizations that wait until the deadline are the ones that miss it.

What we changed

  • Raised text and link contrast to meet the WCAG 2.1 AA minimum ratio, in both light and dark themes.
  • Added a "skip to main content" link and a focusable main landmark for keyboard users.
  • Fixed focus management — the mobile menu leaves the tab order when closed, and focus returns predictably.
  • Converted action controls from placeholder links to real buttons — so they work with both Enter and Space.
  • Corrected ARIA roles, heading order, and landmark structure that automated scanners flag.

Honest footnote: automated tools like axe catch only part of WCAG — roughly a third to a half of all success criteria. Reaching zero automated violations is a milestone, not a finish line. The rest (meaningful alt text, logical reading order, screen-reader labeling) takes manual expertise — which is exactly what a real audit adds, and what we bring to your project.
